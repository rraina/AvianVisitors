## Book these three things now

All three sell out on the door in August, and St Mark's Basilica **abolished its on-site
ticket office in July 2025** — online is the only way in. The Campanile slot is urgent;
it's for tonight.

## Tonight — Monday 10 August

Nothing ticketed is worth starting this late except the Campanile, which happens to be
open until 21:15 and happens to sit directly under a 20:30 sunset. Tonight is the view
and the food; tomorrow is the interiors.

**17:00 — Bags down in Cannaregio.** Forty minutes, no more. Change into something that
covers shoulders and knees — you need that tomorrow at the Basilica, and it saves a
second outfit.

**17:40 — Buy the 24-hour vaporetto pass (€25).** ACTV kiosk at Ferrovia, or the AVM
Venezia Official app. Singles are €9.50, so the pass pays for itself on the third ride
and this plan uses four.
> The clock starts on **first validation, not purchase.** Tap on at 17:45 tonight and
> you're covered until 17:45 tomorrow — comfortably past your run to the station.

**17:50 — Line 1 down the Grand Canal to San Zaccaria (45 min).** Take **Line 1, not
Line 2.** Line 1 is the local — it zigzags to every stop on both banks, which is the
entire point. Line 2 is faster and shows you less. Stand at the open stern.

**18:35 — The Piazzetta, then the Piazza (40 min).** Walk in from the water between the
two columns; this is the front door, the way ambassadors arrived, and almost everyone
now enters from the back. On the Basilica facade look top-left at the **Porta
Sant'Alipio mosaic** — the only original 13th-century mosaic left outside, and it depicts
the Basilica itself with the bronze horses already in place.

**19:15 — One spritz, standing.** For the Caffè Florian experience (open since 1720),
**drink it standing at the interior bar** — a table on the square adds a per-person
orchestra surcharge and roughly triples the bill. Otherwise walk two minutes to Calle dei
Fabbri and pay €4.

**20:05 — Up the Campanile for sunset (45 min).** There's a lift. Red roofs, the whole
lagoon, San Giorgio Maggiore opposite, the Dolomites on the northern horizon if the haze
lifts. Sunset at 20:30 gives you 25 minutes of gold.
> Go up at **20:05, not 19:58**. The bells ring on the hour and at close range they are
> genuinely painful.

**20:55 — Blue hour on the Molo (20 min).** Between ~20:45 and 21:05 the piazza
floodlights come up while the sky is still deep blue rather than black, and San Giorgio
glows across the basin. This is the photograph of the trip, and it's free.

**21:15 — Line 1 back up the Grand Canal, in the dark (30 min).** The return leg is
better than the outbound: the boat is nearly empty and the palazzi are lit from inside,
so you can see into the piano nobile of houses that have been showing off since 1500.
Off at **Ca' d'Oro**. If you miss the last Line 1, San Marco to Cannaregio is a
30-minute walk through emptying streets — no hardship.

**21:45 — Dinner is a bacaro crawl, five minutes from your bed (90 min).** You're staying
on top of the best cicchetti street in Venice, so don't book a restaurant. Eat standing
outside two or three bars in sequence:

- **Vino Vero**, Fondamenta della Misericordia 2497, open to midnight — natural wine and
  the better food. *Baccalà mantecato* on white polenta, *sarde in saor*.
- **Al Timon**, Fondamenta degli Ormesini 2754, open to 01:00 — sixty seconds away,
  grilled *spiedini*, and you can sit on the wooden boat moored in the canal.

Ask for *un'ombra* — a small glass of wine, named for the shade of the Campanile, where
wine sellers once parked their carts to keep it cool. Reckon €25–35 with four glasses.

> **This is the right format for eating alone.** A table for one in a Venetian restaurant
> in August is both hard to get and slightly bleak; a counter where everyone is standing
> is neither. Order two or three things, finish your glass, move to the next bar. Nobody
> will think anything of it, and the Misericordia in August is busy enough at ten at
> night to feel sociable rather than empty.

**23:00 — Bed.** Six and a half hours' sleep. It's the correct trade: the hour you're
buying tomorrow morning is the best hour this city has, and it isn't available at any
other price.

## Tomorrow — Tuesday 11 August

Between six and nine, Venice belongs to residents, delivery barges and you. Then the
ticketed hours begin — and both bookings are timed to a specific reason, not to
convenience.

**06:05 — The dawn walk, north Cannaregio (70 min).** West along the Misericordia, past
last night's bars with the chairs stacked. Then **Campo dei Mori**, with the four stone
Moors built into the wall, and Tintoretto's house a few metres on at Fondamenta dei Mori
3399 — he lived and died in this parish. Loop up to **Madonna dell'Orto**, his church,
for the brick facade in low sun; it doesn't open until mid-morning, so the outside is the
visit. Then south into the **Ghetto Nuovo** — the first ghetto in Europe, 1516, and the
reason the word exists. The buildings are freakishly tall for Venice: confined to one
island, they could only build upward. At 06:30, with nobody in the campo, it lands very
differently than it does at noon.

> **Walking this alone at six in the morning is fine.** Venice has essentially no street
> crime and no traffic, and northern Cannaregio at dawn has bakers, bin barges and
> commuters in it rather than nobody. The real hazards are duller: unlit steps, slick
> algae on the lower fondamente, and GPS being close to useless between tall buildings.
> Screenshot the route before you go, and if you get turned around, follow the yellow
> *Per Rialto* signs painted on the walls.

**07:15 — The two-euro gondola.** At Ca' d'Oro, find the **Santa Sofia traghetto** — a
stripped-out gondola ferrying locals straight across the Grand Canal. €2, sixty seconds,
and you stand up for it. Roughly 07:30–13:00. The only honest gondola ride in the city.
If it hasn't started, walk on to Rialto and lose nothing.

**07:35 — Rialto market, and today is a market day (40 min).** The **Pescaria** runs
Tuesday to Saturday only, ~07:30–13:00. You arrived on a Tuesday, which is lucky — a day
earlier it would have been shuttered. Sea bass, spider crab, langoustines still moving;
the neighbouring Erberia does produce from the lagoon islands.
> Coffee standing at the bar costs about a third of the same coffee at a table. Order
> *un caffè* and a brioche, drink it upright, pay €2.50, move on.

**08:20 — Rialto Bridge, then Piazza San Marco, both nearly empty (40 min).** At 08:20
there will be perhaps a dozen people on the Rialto Bridge; by 10:00 it's shoulder to
shoulder and stays that way until dark. Walk the Mercerie down and arrive in the Piazza
around 08:45, while it's still being swept and the café orchestras haven't set up. Take
the photograph now.

**09:00 — Doge's Palace, first entry (105 min).** Be on the 09:00 dot: state rooms close
to empty, and you're finished before the heat. Courtyard → **Scala d'Oro** →
institutional chambers → **Sala del Maggior Consiglio**, with Tintoretto's *Paradise*,
the largest oil painting in the world, under a frieze of seventy-six doges. Find the one
portrait painted out in black: Marin Falier, beheaded for conspiracy in 1355, his face
erased by decree. Then the Bridge of Sighs and the new prisons.
> Your ticket also covers Museo Correr, the Archaeological Museum and the Marciana
> Library rooms. You have no time for them. Skip them without guilt.

**10:45 — Sit down. Drink water (45 min).** Mid-August runs 28–33 °C with high humidity
and you've been awake since 05:45. There are ~120 public drinking fountains in Venice;
the water is cold, free and safe. If you're restless, the Correr's upper windows look
straight down the length of the Piazza and you're already paid up.

**11:30 — St Mark's Basilica, at exactly the right hour (75 min).** This slot is the most
deliberate decision in the plan. The mosaics — some 8,000 m² of gold — are **only
floodlit Mon–Fri, 11:30–12:45 and 14:00–16:30.** Outside those windows the interior runs
on daylight through small high windows and the ceiling reads as a dim brown haze. A 09:30
visit would have been the classic mistake.

Take the **Museum & Loggia dei Cavalli** add-on: the four original bronze horses, looted
from Constantinople in 1204 (the ones outside are copies), and the balcony over the
Piazza — the second-best view in Venice and the one almost everybody misses.
> **No backpacks, no bare shoulders, no shorts above the knee.** Large bags go to the
> free deposit round the corner at Ateneo San Basso, costing you fifteen minutes you
> don't have. Travel with nothing.

**13:00 — Lunch at All'Arco (45 min).** Back to Rialto, into **All'Arco** (San Polo 436):
a counter, no seats to speak of, cicchetti at €1.50–4 built from what was on the market
stalls forty metres away this morning. Order whatever's on the counter plus something
raw. Sixty seconds away, **Cantina Do Mori** (San Polo 429) has been pouring since 1462
and is the fallback.
> All'Arco stops serving around 14:00–14:30 and does not linger. Don't drift here.

**13:50 — Strada Nova to the hotel, then Santa Lucia (50 min).** Collect bags, continue
over the Ponte delle Guglie to the station — ~15 minutes on foot, or one vaporetto stop,
which your pass still covers. Be on the concourse by **14:45**; Santa Lucia in August is
chaos and platforms only go up about ten minutes before departure.

**15:20 — Frecciarossa or Italo to Bologna Centrale (~1h50).** Around thirty-five trains
a day; fastest 1h33, most closer to 1h50, putting you in Bologna by ~17:10.
> **Book this seat tonight, not at the station.** Walk-up high-speed fares run three to
> four times the advance price and mid-August departures do sell out. I could not reach
> the live timetable, so confirm the exact departure on the Trenitalia or Italo app.

## After Venice — Bologna, then Modena

Landing at 17:10 gives you until ~21:00, and you arrive precisely at aperitivo hour,
which is when Bologna is at its best.

- **Walk in via Via dell'Indipendenza** — fifteen minutes from Centrale to Piazza
  Maggiore, under porticoes the whole way.
- **The Quadrilatero** (Via Pescherie Vecchie, Via Drapperie) — the medieval market
  quarter, Bologna's answer to the Rialto. Mortadella, tortellini, Pignoletto drunk
  standing in the lane at 18:30.
- **San Petronio** on the square — still unfinished after six centuries, with Cassini's
  1655 meridian line inlaid down the north aisle, the longest in the world.
- **Assume the Asinelli tower is shut.** Closed to climbers since late 2023 while the
  leaning Garisenda next door is stabilised; the 2026 works are still unscheduled. Check
  bolognawelcome.com before building an evening around it.
- **Eat tagliatelle al ragù** — and do not ask for spaghetti bolognese. The dish does not
  exist here.

Bologna → **Modena** is a 20–25 minute regionale, ~€4–5, running frequently until late.
A 21:30 or even 22:30 departure is comfortable, so the Bologna end is genuinely elastic.

## If it goes wrong — what to cut, in order

| If this happens | Do this |
|---|---|
| Campanile shut for wind or storm (it closes in bad weather, without notice) | Line 2 to Palanca; sunset from the Zattere or the Skyline rooftop on Giudecca. Free entry, drinks ~€18. |
| You sleep through the alarm | You've lost the best hour and can't get it back. Keep the 11:30 Basilica, drop the Doge's Palace — the Basilica is the one that can't be rescheduled. |
| Departure firms back up to a hard 14:00 | Drop the Doge's Palace, not the Basilica. Dawn walk, market, 11:30 Basilica with the Loggia, then straight to the station from Rialto. |
| You want the Doge's Palace *Secret Itineraries* (Casanova's cell, the torture chamber, €29) | English tours start ~09:55 and run 90 minutes, colliding with your 11:30. Worth it only if you move the Basilica to 14:00 and push the train back an hour. |
| Hotel checkout is before you leave | Every Cannaregio hotel will hold bags for the day. Left luggage at Santa Lucia is the paid fallback, ~€6. |

## Calibrate this to your front door

Cannaregio runs a good kilometre and a half end to end, so "Cannaregio" on its own is
worth about ten minutes either way. Find your row and apply the adjustment — the sequence
never changes, only the departure times.

| If you're near… | Ferrovia | Misericordia | Rialto | Adjust |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Lista di Spagna / Santa Lucia** (west end, by the station) | 5 min | 18 min | 25 min | Leave for dinner 21:35. Easiest station run tomorrow — leave the hotel at 14:25. |
| **Strada Nova / Ca' d'Oro** (the middle — most likely) | 12 min | 8 min | 8 min | Run the plan exactly as written. This is what the times assume. |
| **Misericordia / Ormesini** (the bacaro street itself) | 15 min | 2 min | 14 min | Best case. Dinner is downstairs and the dawn walk starts at your door — lie in until 06:15. |
| **Fondamente Nove / Sant'Alvise** (north and east edge) | 22 min | 12 min | 16 min | Add 10 min to every leg. Start the dawn walk at 05:55 and run it in reverse — Ghetto first, Madonna dell'Orto last. |
